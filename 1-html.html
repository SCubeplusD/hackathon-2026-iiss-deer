Ethics

  • Guidance, not diagnosis. Arm-band results and diet gaps trigger a referral message. A health worker or clinician decides.
  • Privacy by design. No names are collected. Only aggregated, anonymized counts go to the planner, and small groups should be hidden.
  • Local ownership. Data should stay under local control, with consent explained in the local language and by audio.
  • Access. Built for cheap phones and low literacy: icons, big buttons, offline use.

Honest limits of this prototype

  • Nutrient values and prices are illustrative. Replace them with FAO/INFOODS composition tables and local market prices before real use.
  • The fix uses a greedy cheapest-per-gap method, a stand-in for full linear programming.
  • Requirements are simplified. A nutritionist must validate them.
  • District figures are synthetic, not real children's data.
